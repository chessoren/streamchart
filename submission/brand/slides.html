StreamChart

Every patient has a chart.
Now every stream does.

A health record and a care loop for every urban stream: a 60-second citizen check-up, a sealed AI second look, confidence grades, a care plan co-signed by an ecologist — and a reply to the citizen. Exported as HL7 FHIR R4.

The problem

Citizen science gives a lot — and never hears back.

01

No continuous thread

Observations pile up without a story. Nobody can say “this stream was better in May, relapsed in August, here is why”.

02

No measured trust

A child's photo and an ecologist's survey land in the same database with the same apparent weight. Authorities can't tell how far to trust either.

03

No reply

The person who gave their time receives nothing, so they don't come back. Track 5 names it: low repeat engagement.

OneAquaHealth has built the organs of a health system for urban streams — citizen app, city dashboards, resilience map, GEOSSIP, a decision-support system, DipteraCAST. What is missing is the patient.

How it works

One loop: observe, understand, act, come back.

1 · Triage

60-second check-up

One photo, seven big questions. The citizen answers first; the AI's look is sealed.

2 · Record

The stream's chart

State, pulse, timeline. Every fact carries its author and its grade.

3 · Assess

Hypothesis + risk

Cautious hypotheses and a 7-day, weather-aware health indication.

4 · Treat

Co-signed care plan

Measures from the OAH catalogue. Official only when an ecologist signs.

5 · Follow up

Reply & remission

Citizens hear what their check-up changed. A follow-up confirms recovery.

◌ Not followed→⧗ Under watch→! Alert→✓ Plan validated→♥ Remission

Track 3 · AI that assists, never replaces

The citizen answers first. The AI's answer is sealed.

  • Gemini vision looks at the photo while the citizen answers — it never sees their answers.
  • Its answer is hashed (SHA-256) on arrival and revealed only after the citizen seals their own: commit–reveal.
  • Differences are highlighted on the photo. Keep mine, adopt the AI's, or ask a second opinion. The last word is human.
  • The AI may say “unsure” — and always does for odour.

Measured trust

Every fact carries a confidence grade.

APhoto, person and AI agree, corroborated by others — or validated by an expert
BPhoto with person–AI agreement, or two concordant people
CPlausible but isolated, or an unresolved disagreement
DFragile: no photo, imprecise or contradicted

The grade is a property of the information, not a judgement of the person. It moves: a C becomes B when a neighbour confirms it, and A when the referent ecologist validates it.

The community becomes more credible than the sum of its parts.

Disagreements are shown, never smoothed. A proposed method — validation against expert assessments is on the roadmap.

Track 2 · Data to insight

A stream's whole life, on one page.

  • Identity, state badge and one sentence anyone can read.
  • A pulse: a heartbeat trace of its health over four months.

Track 6 · The One Health bridge

From a stream's symptoms to a signal for people.

  • Hypotheses, never diagnoses: “possible input of wastewater or polluted runoff — confidence medium”, with reasons and what would confirm it.
  • Seven-day indication: stagnant water × a live Open-Meteo forecast → conditions favourable to mosquitoes. Explained, dated, labelled “not an epidemiological forecast”.
  • A slot is ready for DipteraCAST (ENORA) as the chart's “laboratory analysis”.
StreamChart8 / 13
Human in the loop, made visible

The AI proposes. An ecologist signs.

  • Care plans of at most five lines, quoted from the OneAquaHealth D2.4 Catalogue of measures (§4.2.2 Sewer system and point-source improvements…).
  • Each line: what, why, who (city, association, residents), urgency, effort.
  • The plan stays proposed until the referent ecologist removes what is premature and signs — the stamp goes from proposed to validated.
  • The city's ward room shows where to go first, in three lines.

Tracks 4 & 5 · The loop closes

“Your check-up triggered an inspection.”

Every contributor hears what their check-up changed: compared, first in the chain, plan validated, intervention done, follow-up, remission. No points, no streaks — the only reward that lasts is seeing your gesture have an effect.

And the stream speaks, in the first person, in three registers — grounded sentence by sentence in its record. Tap a sentence, see its source. It never accuses, never alarms, never says the water is safe.

Track 7 · Digital health standards

The chart is an HL7 FHIR R4 record.

The streamPatient + Location
60-second formQuestionnaire / QuestionnaireResponse
Each answer · photoObservation (+grade) · Media
The AI's lookObservation from a Device
Who did what · sealsProvenance + signature
HypothesisDiagnosticReport
7-day indicationRiskAssessment
Care plan · co-signatureCarePlan · Provenance (verifier)
Reply to the citizenCommunication

One click exports a transaction Bundle — and posts it to the public HAPI FHIR R4 server, which accepts it (43 resources created, re-posts idempotent).

Making the stream a Patient is the metaphor made literal: CarePlan, DiagnosticReport and RiskAssessment need a Patient subject, so any FHIR server, viewer or pipeline handles a river with no custom resource.

Feasibility & scale

No hardware. No budget. Five cities tomorrow.

A sign per stream

A printed “patient file” with a QR code — and the same viewpoint for every photo.

A school kit

One page, 45 minutes, four roles. Any school adopts a stream in a week.

Built on OAH

Their indicators, their catalogue, their tools as “labs”. A layer, not a competitor.

Static & open

React + TypeScript, no server, BYO Gemini key, MIT. Any city can fork it.

Benevento · Coimbra · Ghent · Oslo · Toulouse — a seasonal Stream Cup on what matters: streams followed, remissions, quality, schools.
